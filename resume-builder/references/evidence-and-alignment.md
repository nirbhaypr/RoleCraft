# Evidence and alignment

## Recover the current state

Start with supplied history, existing resume sources, prior candidate answers, and the latest accepted outputs. A newer explicit correction supersedes older research or a draft worksheet. Keep a short evidence/decision record outside the resume, including unresolved questions and which version is current.

For each substantial accomplishment, capture what is available:

- Business problem, users, and production or pilot status.
- What the candidate personally implemented or owned, separately from team or product scope.
- Technical mechanism and confirmed tools.
- Outcome: time, quality, cost, adoption, scale, reliability, or an observable qualitative benefit.
- Relevant role or timeframe and the source of the claim.

Ask focused questions about the strongest projects instead of requiring a complete questionnaire before drafting. Approximate evidence can be useful when identified as approximate. Optional contact details or portfolio links need not block a supported draft. An inaccessible profile is not a source of additional career claims.

## Research real target jobs

For market research, use **job search** to discover relevant openings and **page extraction** or **web research** to obtain full descriptions. When the user supplies target descriptions, use those directly and retrieve only missing or time-sensitive information needed for the request. Reuse full descriptions already returned by a search integration instead of paying for redundant detail requests.

Apply target location, remote eligibility, role family, and seniority constraints. A job labeled remote may still restrict country or work authorization. Evaluate responsibility and required experience rather than inferring seniority solely from labels such as Associate or Senior.

Keep enough provenance to revisit findings: title, employer, location/eligibility, source URL, retrieval date, description, and availability status. Deduplicate by stable listing identity or canonical URL, with employer/title/location as a fallback. Separate retrieved, relevant, excluded, and independently verified counts.

For openings used as application recommendations, check employer-hosted pages where possible. An indexed snippet or old third-party listing does not establish that a vacancy is open. Mark inaccessible or closed postings accordingly; they may still illustrate technical requirements but should not appear as verified current opportunities.

Use a representative set across the requested role families. A listing or spending cap is a maximum, not a quota. Stop when the relevant descriptions adequately establish recurring requirements and additional retrieval adds little. Respect the authorized budget and access scope. If an integration cannot proceed, retain usable results and use another available capability within scope; do not broaden account permissions or keep retrying the same failure.

Summarize recurring requirements by role family and distinguish required from preferred qualifications. If counting mentions, state the sample and denominator; a tool's appearance is not necessarily a mandatory requirement. Avoid presenting a selected sample as a comprehensive labor-market survey.

## Use actual resumes as editorial references

When this research is requested or useful, inspect real public resumes with original-source provenance, such as an owner's portfolio-linked document. Keep these separate from fictional samples, template pages, and recruiter advice. If a document cannot be opened, do not count it as inspected.

Record the transferable pattern and its limits: understandable business capabilities, concrete implementation detail, outcome framing, categorized skills, or delivery responsibility. Do not copy another person's achievements, stack, metrics, or contact data. Public availability does not verify the author's claims or prove hiring success. Reference resumes can suggest questions and presentation choices, not facts about this candidate.

## Convert evidence into positioning

Use a compact requirement-to-evidence map when the research is substantial:

| Target requirement | Candidate evidence | Resume treatment | Remaining gap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A responsibility or skill from reviewed jobs | Confirmed work and its source | Existing bullet, accurate rewording, or confirmed skill | A specific question or a genuine missing capability |

Keep the skills section specific enough to be useful: name confirmed technologies in conventional categories, and substantiate the most important capabilities in experience. Knowledge of a technique does not establish that the candidate deployed it. Do not infer adjacent technologies, formal leadership, production ownership, client responsibility, or architecture scope from a related activity.

Write bullets with a useful combination of **business purpose → contribution/mechanism → outcome or scope**. Not every bullet needs a number or the same sentence formula. Distinguish accomplishments that demonstrate different capabilities; eliminate repeated framing without collapsing a substantial portfolio into a few generic statements.

For promotion history, retain exact titles and dates and clarify functional specialization where supported. Ask about changed responsibilities and project timing when needed. If the candidate authorizes an editorial allocation by relevance, record that basis and avoid inventing completion dates. Do not imply that a short recent role produced the entire portfolio. Put aggregate impact where it will not be misattributed to a single role or short period. A target job title is not a previously held title.

Keep metrics faithful. Preserve approximations, peak-use qualifiers, ranges, and the supplied denominator. A saving of several hours does not become hours per query or per week without evidence. Do not multiply portfolio adoption by every feature or transfer a metric between projects. If asked for a believable number, request a defensible baseline; if none exists, state the qualitative benefit. Do not adjust supplied numbers merely to make them sound stronger or more conservative.

Create distinct role variants when warranted by the requirements: one might emphasize technical depth and another stakeholder scoping, integrations, and delivery. Keep employment facts and outcomes consistent across variants. Tailor selection, order, terminology, and emphasis rather than inventing new experience.
