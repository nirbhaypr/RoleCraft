# Resume Creation Workflow

Turn a candidate's actual experience into targeted, readable resumes with reproducible sources and verified outputs. Preserve successful decisions as the work progresses; a later design change should not undo accepted content.

## Enter at the requested stage

Inspect the latest source, deliverables, research, and candidate corrections before starting. Identify the requested roles, geography and remote eligibility, output formats, and existing content/design decisions. For a narrow edit or audit, perform that stage and its affected checks without restarting the whole workflow.

Read the active candidate's saved preferences for targeting when available. Keep role/location preferences, current or expected compensation, notice period, and other practical screening details separate from resume content and career evidence. A profile-only request belongs to candidate-profile when available and must not regenerate or edit a resume. A changed preference may guide a later requested resume revision, but is not a new career fact.

Use capabilities available in the environment: **job search**, **web research**, **page extraction**, **document generation**, **PDF rendering**, and **text extraction**. Choose an appropriate integration or local tool for each capability. No particular service, scraper, renderer, or external job identifier is required. Use relevant format-specific skills when available; keep the cross-format checks below regardless of implementation.

Ask only for missing information that materially changes the result. Continue independent research or drafting while awaiting clarification. Record answers and superseded decisions so the candidate is not asked the same question again. User-selected page length, section order, template, typography, and palette are task preferences, not universal resume rules.

## Build the content

Read [Evidence and alignment](references/evidence-and-alignment.md) for a new resume, job-market review, reference-resume research, or substantive content revision.

1. **Establish candidate evidence.** Capture employment and promotion dates, formal titles, education, personal contribution, technology actually used, and defensible outcomes. Distinguish confirmed facts, candidate estimates, editorial framing, and unanswered questions. Preserve units, timeframes, and qualifiers on metrics.
2. **Research the target.** Review full, relevant job descriptions with geography and seniority constraints. Use an available job search integration and verify important openings against employer pages. Separate current opportunities from historical technical references. When useful or requested, inspect actual public resumes for writing patterns separately from template research.
3. **Map requirements to evidence.** Identify strong matches, wording improvements, questions to confirm, and genuine gaps. Job requirements suggest what to investigate; they do not become candidate experience automatically. Treat frequency counts as sample observations, not ATS scores.
4. **Draft and refine.** Write around the business problem, personal technical contribution, and supported outcome or scope. Replace opaque internal project names with understandable capabilities. Show progression across roles while preserving formal history. Create multiple variants only when different target roles benefit from different emphasis.
5. **Maintain one authoritative content source.** Use structured data or an equivalent editable source for shared facts and variant wording. Apply corrections there, then regenerate affected deliverables. Do not over-compress substantive experience to fit an arbitrary page count or inflate it with repeated tool lists.

Content is ready for production when each claim has a basis, the latest corrections are incorporated, and remaining unknowns can be omitted or accurately qualified. Use existing authorization to proceed; do not introduce a compulsory approval gate for every stage.

## Produce and refine the documents

Read [Design and production](references/design-and-production.md) when choosing a template, generating files, or changing layout or colour.

Preserve the selected design or provide a small, practical comparison when a choice is requested. Use a straightforward reading order, ordinary text, visible contact links, and recognizable sections. Build requested formats from the same content; editable companions may need format-specific layout choices.

Keep content revisions separate from presentation revisions. During a design-only change, preserve wording and facts. Generate requested alternatives from the accepted baseline; after selection, propagate the chosen design to sources and all requested outputs. Retain the previous accepted revision before replacing it.

## Verify and deliver

Read [Verification](references/verification.md) before delivering generated documents or performing a parsing audit.

Check the actual final files in two ways: inspect rendered pages and compare complete extracted content with the authoritative source. Render editable documents independently; a native PDF does not prove that the Word version paginates correctly. Recheck affected outputs after changes. For a dedicated ATS audit, use independent parser engines and preserve evidence of both passes and limitations.

Deliver clearly labeled current files, the editable source, and a concise account of what changed and what was verified. Keep research, unanswered questions, and operational notes outside submitted resumes. Distinguish visual quality, text completeness, reading order, and employer ATS behavior; local checks establish only what was actually tested. An audit-only request should leave the resume unchanged.

When the user asks to record a tailored resume in their application tracker, use application-tracker if available and link the exact prepared version to the matching opening. Preserve the separate record of previously submitted versions; generating or recommending a new resume does not establish its submission or change application stage.
