# Question-specific guidance

Read the sections relevant to the current question. These patterns guide evidence selection, not a mandatory response template.

## Motivation, company interest and career direction

For “What interests you about this role?”, choose a specific responsibility or working style from the description and connect it to documented work or an established aspiration. Distinguish prior experience from the next application of it: internal stakeholder collaboration can support interest in direct customer work without becoming prior external-client ownership.

For “Why this company?”, use a verified product, problem or mission only when it adds substance. Avoid claims of being a longtime customer, admiration for a founder, cultural fit or personal passion without evidence. A reason for leaving, willingness to change industries, or long-term career commitment is a candidate preference to recover or ask about, not infer from applying.

## Fit, strengths and differentiators

Connect the most important requirements to concrete examples of contribution and outcome. Prefer two strong matches over repeating every job-description keyword. Use relevant terminology only where supported. Do not inflate tenure to satisfy the listing, equate a senior title with management, or imply that interest in a domain establishes prior domain expertise.

If directly asked about a missing requirement, answer honestly and describe relevant transferable experience where useful. A general fit answer need not add an unsolicited apology for every gap.

## Projects, architecture and technical depth

Explain the problem, personal contribution, relevant system flow or design decision, and supported result. Include tradeoffs or failure handling only where the candidate has supplied them, or explicitly distinguish a proposed approach from past implementation.

Keep project boundaries intact. If the evidence confirms that orchestration, retrieval, memory and quality improvements belonged to the same system, they can form one end-to-end account. That confirmation does not establish an unreported hosting stack, memory-store vendor, scale, latency target or evaluation dataset. Skill-list entries alone do not fill these gaps.

For a hypothetical design or case question, state necessary assumptions and explain the proposed reasoning. Use conditional language so a sensible design recommendation does not become a claim that the candidate has already built it. For a factual technical question, answer it directly; verify version-sensitive details with primary sources when needed.

## Behavioral examples, challenges, failure and leadership

Use a real incident and naturally cover situation, personal action and result; explicit STAR headings are optional. Preserve the distinction between individual work and team outcomes. Explain lessons only when supported by the candidate's account or clearly offered as suggested reflection for their review.

A successful project bullet does not establish a conflict, outage, failure, deadline crisis or leadership episode. If a specific incident is missing, ask for the minimal story: what happened, what the candidate did, what changed, and what they learned. Draft other supported answers in the meantime. Never invent an anecdote to make the answer memorable.

## Experience, yes/no and numeric screening

Answer against the exact wording and supplied options. Relevant experience, professional employment, management experience and years with a named tool are different quantities. Month-only employment dates permit approximate tenure, not an invented start day; near a mandatory boundary, ask for the detail that matters.

For a numeric field, respect its unit and precision and do not round upward to imply eligibility. If a required choice cannot represent the truthful answer, explain that mismatch outside the field rather than selecting a convenient false value. Missing evidence remains unknown, not automatically “No.”

## Compensation, availability and work arrangements

Recover the latest explicit preference or fact. Separate current compensation, expected compensation and the employer's advertised budget. Preserve currency, annual/monthly basis, fixed/base pay, variable pay, equity and total compensation. Do not translate a job-search target into an accepted offer or a fixed commitment. If asked to recommend a range, distinguish researched advice from the candidate's chosen application answer.

Notice period is not necessarily the earliest joining date: buyout, resignation status and negotiability need evidence. Location does not establish relocation, travel, shift or on-site willingness. Recheck time-sensitive facts when context suggests a change; do not ask again merely because a new question repeats a known answer.

## Eligibility, declarations and personal fields

Use explicit candidate answers for work authorization, sponsorship, citizenship, disability, demographic self-identification, conflicts, background declarations or similar fields. Do not infer these from a name, address, education or employment history. Keep work authorization and sponsorship requirements distinct. Do not choose “prefer not to disclose” on the candidate's behalf.

When a necessary answer is unknown, ask the candidate and leave that item clearly pending while completing the rest. Retain sensitive responses only as needed for the authorized task or an explicitly maintained profile; do not copy them into the reusable skill or unrelated application notes.

## Additional information and unusual prompts

For an optional “Anything else?” field, add relevant evidence or context not already covered. If nothing useful remains, recommend leaving it blank rather than repeating the resume. For creative or values questions, ground the answer in the candidate's actual preferences; ask when the choice would otherwise invent a personal belief. Respect the requested format without manufacturing personality or biography.
